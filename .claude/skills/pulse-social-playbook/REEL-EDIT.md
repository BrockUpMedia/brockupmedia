# Reel edit craft (Liza, 2026-10-21)

When cutting any Reel, work as one of the best film editors in the world: someone who knows that
the cut and the music, not the caption, are what move people. Every choice serves one feeling. For
Lights On that feeling is "keep going, never give up": growth, belonging, confidence arriving.

## Find the story before cutting

- Watch every clip in the folder first. Log the moments, not the shots: a first nervous glance, a
  laugh between takes, a shoulder dropping as someone relaxes, a proud look at the monitor, a high
  five, a person owning the frame.
- Pick one small arc for 15 to 30 seconds: **arrive, try, find it, own it.** Week 8 headshot day,
  for example: the empty studio and lights going up, a participant stepping onto the mark, small
  adjustments and a laugh, then the final confident look straight down the lens.
- Faces and eyes carry emotion. Favour close and medium shots where you can read a face. Use wides
  only to set the room and give the edit air.
- Never use a moment that could read as pity, struggle for its own sake or someone being "helped".
  Show professionals at work and friends in a room.

## Structure (with music in mind)

1. **Hook, 0 to 1.5s:** the most striking frame first, already in motion, with a bold text line on
   screen. No logos, no slow fade in.
2. **Build, about 1.5 to 12s:** short cuts that quicken as the music builds. Context shots, hands,
   lights, the slate, small smiles. Each cut answers a question the last shot raised.
3. **Lift, the emotional peak:** land the strongest moment (the confident look, the laugh, the
   whole group together) exactly where the music lifts or drops. Hold it longer than feels safe,
   1.5 to 2.5s. This shot is why the Reel exists.
4. **Resolve, last 3 to 5s:** slow down, one or two held shots, a closing text line and the
   brand end card (dark `#0D0D0D`, yellow `#FFD600`): "LIGHTS ON", "Inclusive by design", "@lightsonqld". End on a clean beat so the loop restarts
   smoothly; Reels replay, so the last frame should flow into the first.

## Cutting rules

- Cut to a tempo. Build every Reel on a 120 BPM grid (one beat every 0.5s) unless the pack says
  otherwise: build cuts on every 2 beats (1s), quicker cuts on every beat into the lift, held shots
  for 3 to 5 beats after it. Instagram lets Liza pick which part of a song plays, so the pack tells
  her where the lift needs to land (for example "start the song so the chorus hits at 0:12").
- Cut on motion: a head turn, a step, a hand rising. Movement hides the cut and keeps energy up.
- Trim every clip hard. Start a shot just after the action begins and leave just before it ends.
- Vary shot size from cut to cut (wide, close, medium). Never two near-identical frames in a row.
- Light speed ramps or a slow motion beat only on the peak moment, never as decoration.
- Gentle, consistent grade across clips: match white balance and exposure, lift shadows slightly,
  keep skin tones true. No heavy filters.
- Keep the subject inside the Reel safe zone: nothing important in the top 250px or bottom 400px
  of the 1080x1920 frame, where the Instagram interface sits.

## Text and captions

- Original audio is always stripped, spoken audio included, ever (Liza, 2026-10-22), so on-screen
  text carries the story. On teaching clips the advice becomes full sentence captions. Open with "[Upbeat
  music]" for Deaf and screen reader viewers.
- Three to five short text lines in total, each on screen long enough to read twice (about 1.5s
  per five words). Sans serif, high contrast, on a neutral band, no wider than about two thirds of
  the frame, inside the safe zone.
- Text states what is happening and lets the pictures do the feeling. Never "brave", "inspiring"
  or "overcome".

## Music brief in the pack

For every Reel, the pack gives Liza:

- **Mood:** for example "building piano into a big warm beat, hopeful, anthemic".
- **Tempo:** about 120 BPM, which the cut is built on.
- **Where the lift lands:** the second mark of the peak shot, so she sets the song start in the app.
- **Search words** for Instagram's library: "uplifting", "motivational", "inspiring piano",
  "feel good", "anthem".

## Final check before handing over

Watch it through three times: once for story (does it move you by the peak?), once with the sound
off (does the text alone make sense?), once for craft (cuts on motion, no flash frames, safe zones,
loop is clean). If the peak doesn't land, recut before showing Liza.
